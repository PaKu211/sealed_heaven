# Game Vision — Sealed Heaven

Version 0.2-draft · 17 November 2026

## One-sentence pitch

A systemic Eastern Fantasy sandbox RPG where a player begins as one person in an independently living world, and work, relationships, cultivation, exploration, and conflict create lasting consequences.

## Player fantasy

The player is not forced to be a chosen savior or eventual ruler. Starting backgrounds create different access, debts, skills, possessions, allies and enemies. A player may remain a wandering martial artist, become a craftsperson, recruit a small party, join a sect, or eventually establish an organization/settlement. Increasing social scale is possible, not mandatory progression.

## Design pillars

1. World autonomy — NPCs and institutions pursue needs and goals even without the player nearby.
2. Personal persistence — identity, injuries, ownership, relationships and important history survive unloading and saving.
3. Agency across scale — individual and organizational play emerge from actions rather than a fixed mission ladder.
4. Readable causality — players get meaningful feedback; developers can inspect exact reasons and transaction history.
5. Eastern fantasy with generic foundations — Qi, sects and techniques are data-driven rules over generic domain primitives.
6. Production restraint — scope, visual fidelity and simulation granularity are chosen for playability and solo development.

## Inspiration map (principles, not copies)

| Reference | Desired lesson | Explicit avoidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kenshi | Freeform lives, factions, party growth | Real-time micromanagement complexity in M0 |
| Caves of Qud | Interacting systems and readable roguelike map | Copying lore, assets, interface or mechanics exactly |
| Dwarf Fortress | Historical continuity, world not centered on player | Complete civilization simulator in M0 |
| RimWorld | NPC needs, jobs and social consequences | Mandatory colony-management game loop |
| Songs of Syx | Physical logistics and scalable abstraction | Tens-of-thousands fully active agents |
| Of Tales and Deeds | Minimalist sandbox UX, keyboard play, procedural portraits | Importing proprietary assets/code or assuming exact engine internals |

## First playable promise (M1)

Within a short session, a novice cultivator can inspect a living settlement, move and talk, gather herbs, perform a paid job, trade, train a small ability, affect one relationship, and encounter an explainable local dispute. Time can pause; actions and events persist. The game should communicate *what changed and why*.

## Audience and access

Players who enjoy emergent sandbox RPGs, roguelikes, simulations, exploration and martial-arts fantasy. Desktop keyboard-first with mouse support. A low-end development laptop (about 4 GB RAM, integrated Intel graphics) is a performance test target, not a demonstrated supported configuration yet.

## Explicit non-goals through M1

- No runtime LLM/generative AI for NPCs, content or portraits; AI agents assist development only.
- No multiplayer, online service, or runtime model API dependency.
- No full Sealed Heaven narrative import or autogenerated quest interpretation.
- No Western Fantasy ruleset or world travel between universes.
- No giant map/worldgen, fully simulated civilization history, sophisticated market equilibrium, wars with thousands of units, or complete cultivation ladder.
- No promise of cross-platform bitwise replay until experimentally validated.
- No copying proprietary sprites, sound, UI layout, dialogue or source from inspiration titles.

## Success definition

Engineering success: reproducible causal living-world state under headless tests and a clean rebuildable Bevy presentation. Product success: the M1 gameplay loop is understandable and interesting in observed playtests. Neither criterion alone is sufficient.

## Open design validations

- Pause-based real-time: adopted for M1 prototype, still subject to playtest.
- M1 host cadence experiment: compare exact 10, 20 and 30 steps per real-time second. Logical ticks and scenario world-time conversion are separate. See [GDD validation](GDD_v0.2.md#12-validation).
- Pixel tile size: 16x16 proposed, verify actual readability.
- First social conflict: the M0 scenario demonstrates a resource trigger causing a declared relationship delta. The M1 playtest uses one readable local consequence. See [M0 causality](M0_ACCEPTANCE_TESTS.md#5-causality-and-performance).
